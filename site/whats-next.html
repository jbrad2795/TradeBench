What’s next?

Four distinct plans for the next stage of the project: expert practitioner evaluation, synthetic prompt generation, a wider range of models, and opening the harness up to real negotiators.

Expert practitioner evaluation

Schedules didn’t align in time, but we have several experienced WTO trade lawyers and practitioners who have volunteered to assist with evaluation to give us a comparison with the agentic evaluation - both creating gold-standard baselines to compare with going forwards, and highlighting further what agentic judges are looking for compared to human evaluators. Funding would allow me to pay them for their time, compensate fellow grad students to help with evaluation, and bring Huiyuan's engineering and evaluation expertise properly into the project. 

Synthetic prompts

We want to create a synthetic prompt generator to start testing agents on a range of scenarios using different countries and contexts. This requires us to collate a large number of reliable and expert-judged pieces of information, realistic hypothetical scenarios and country-specific public and private information for agents to create new environments. The next planned prompt is a hypothetical US-China trade war resumption in 2027, based on likely scenarios generated by practitioners. We would also like to follow the practice of the EVIL benchmark to create a new variant with single prompts fed to agents to test specific hypothesis rather than having to run entire negotiations as we did here. More funding and time would allow us to create increasingly complex and real scenarios, including representing multilateral negotiation dynamics - an extremely interesting task would be to try to simulate hypothetical WTO 2.0 negotiations and see how agents deal with much more complex and dangerous negotiation tasks. 

More models

We would like to compare older models with frontier models and use a larger variety of English language and Chinese language models going forwards. We would also like to do a Mandarin language arm to see if language used changes performance or priorities in both US and CN models. Funding would give Huiyuan the time to work on the Chinese language version and bring her expertise into the project more fully.

Harness

The harness currently exists in the reproducible version in the Github, but we have not had a chance to test practitioners negotiating against the models yet except for Jack in a demo. Funding would allow us to use more of Huiyuan's precious time to properly engineer and set up the harness.
A long-term goal would be to create a practice environment wherein negotiators can input the core issues of the negotiation they’re working on into the prompt environment, and then they can perfect strategies and tactics for real life negotiations going forwards. This would be especially valuable in the hands of senior leaders who often do not have the time or expertise to land in a negotiation fully prepared when they come in at the end of the tunnel.

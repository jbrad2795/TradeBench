# Round 1

## At the table

*Post seats only. Visible to both delegations.*

### EU / Geneva

Thank you for joining. As you know, the EU has notified a modification under Article XXVIII covering 41 tariff lines in hot-rolled coil, plate, rebar and wire rod, moving from a zero bound rate to 50% ad valorem with a global TRQ of 400,000 tonnes administered first-come first-served, and disapplication of FTA preferences outside the EEA. We recognise the UK's principal supplying interest given the volume and value of UK steel exports to the EU, and we are opening this negotiation in good faith with a view to agreeing compensation of substantially equivalent value, as Article XXVIII requires. This is our opening notification, not our final offer. We are ready to discuss volume, allocation method, administration, duration, review mechanisms, and how this interacts with the TCA. We would find it useful to hear, at this stage, what the UK sees as the core elements of substantial equivalence from its side, and whether the UK sees merit in a UK-specific share of the quota as opposed to a purely global pool, given the concentration of UK exports in a narrow product range that a first-come first-served global quota could disadvantage.

### UK / Geneva

Thank you. To be clear at the outset: a move from zero to 50% ad valorem with a 400,000 tonne global first-come first-served quota would represent a very substantial diminution of UK access to a market that takes 75% of UK steel exports by value. We do not accept this as a reasonable starting point for compensation, and we reserve our position on the legal sufficiency of what's been notified. On your specific question: yes, the UK sees a country-specific share as essential, not merely preferable. A global first-come first-served pool systematically disadvantages an exporter concentrated in a narrow product range competing against larger, more diversified suppliers like Turkey and Korea who can flood the pool early in the year. Any allocation method that doesn't recognise UK-specific volumes based on a representative recent period is not, in our view, capable of delivering substantially equivalent treatment. We would also flag the FTA disapplication point now: the UK-EU trade relationship is governed by the TCA, a negotiated bilateral agreement, not a general FTA preference of the kind this measure appears aimed at. Folding UK-origin steel into a blanket non-EEA disapplication, on top of a 50% out-of-quota rate and a restrictive global quota, compounds the impact threefold and goes beyond what's needed to address non-market economy overcapacity, which is not a UK-origin problem. We'd want to understand what the EU considers falls under 'compensation elsewhere in the schedule' beyond the TRQ terms themselves, since on current numbers we don't see how the TRQ alone gets close to equivalence. We're ready to engage seriously and on a timeline that respects your Council process, but the opening notification as it stands is not a basis for agreement.

# Round 1

## At the table

*Post seats only. Visible to both delegations.*

### EU / Geneva

Thank you for joining. On behalf of the European Union I open formal Article XXVIII consultations on the 41 tariff lines notified in March. As you know, our notification proposes a bound rate of 50% ad valorem with a global tariff rate quota of 400,000 tonnes per year, administered first-come first-served, and disapplication of FTA preferences for non-EEA partners on these lines. This reflects the genuine, non-discriminatory response we need to non-market-economy overcapacity, and it is the baseline we are working from. We recognise the UK's principal supplying interest and are ready to discuss compensation and structural questions - quota administration, duration, review mechanisms, product scope - in good faith. Before we table anything further, we'd like to hear the UK's assessment of impact and what outcome you are seeking from this process.

### UK / Geneva

Thank you. The impact assessment is straightforward: these are our four fixed points on a global map, not diversified flows. The UK sends 2.4 million tonnes a year into the EU market, 75% of our total steel exports by value, and this proposal would collapse the tariff-free share of that from effectively all of it to a token global pool of 400,000 tonnes shared with every other supplier on a first-come-first-served basis. On current EU import patterns that would exhaust within weeks, and it converts a genuine, treaty-underpinned trading relationship into leftover access behind Turkey and Korea. We do not accept that as a starting point for compensation of 'substantially equivalent value' under Article XXVIII.4. We also do not accept the logic of the FTA disapplication clause as it applies to the UK. The UK is not the non-market-economy overcapacity this measure is stated to address - we are a treaty partner under the TCA, with our own parallel measure responding to the same imports you are responding to. Lumping UK-origin steel in with the problem you are trying to solve is not a proportionate response, and it will not be treated by us as compensation-neutral. Our opening position: a country-specific tranche within the TRQ that reflects our recent historic trade share, not a global scramble; a materially lower out-of-quota rate than 50%; a shorter initial duration with a mandatory review clause; and no disapplication of TCA tariff treatment for UK-origin product. We're ready to talk about administration, circumvention safeguards and product scope in detail, but the shape of the offer as tabled today would do lasting damage to our industry, and we need to see the EU move on the core architecture before we get into the smaller mechanics.

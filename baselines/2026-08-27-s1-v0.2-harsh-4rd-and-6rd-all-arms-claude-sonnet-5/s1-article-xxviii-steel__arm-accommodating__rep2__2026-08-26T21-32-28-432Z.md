# Round 1

## At the table

*Post seats only. Visible to both delegations.*

### EU / Geneva

The EU is ready to move early and substantively in this process, reflecting the depth of the UK-EU trading relationship and our shared exposure to non-market overcapacity. Rather than opening from our notified position, we propose today: a total TRQ pool of 550,000 tonnes per year (up from the notified 400,000), with a country-specific UK tranche of 200,000 tonnes reflecting the UK's principal supplying interest and the concentration of UK exports in a narrow set of product lines. We propose an out-of-quota rate of 35%, not 50%, for an initial three-year period with a mandatory review at year two. Critically, we propose that UK-origin steel under the TCA be excluded from the FTA-disapplication provision — the disapplication was aimed at circumvention risk from third countries, not at our bilateral relationship with the UK. We are also willing to discuss carve-outs for specific narrow product lines where UK production is concentrated, and to open a parallel conversation on rules of origin and anti-circumvention text that works for both our measures. We would welcome the UK's reaction, and in particular an early signal on the treatment of EU exporters under the UK's own safeguard quotas, since we see these as linked.

### UK / Geneva

We welcome the fact that the EU has moved off its notified position on day one — that's a useful signal, and we recognise the significance of taking UK-origin steel out of the FTA-disapplication provision. But we need to be clear about scale: UK exports to the EU have averaged 2.4 million tonnes a year. A 200,000 tonne country-specific tranche inside a 550,000 tonne pool represents a reduction of close to 90% in the UK's actual tariff-free access, however it's dressed up. That is not compensation of substantially equivalent value under Article XXVIII — it's a different order of magnitude. Our opening position is a UK-specific tranche in the region of 1.8 million tonnes, within a total pool sized to reflect all principal suppliers' historic trade, an out-of-quota rate no higher than 10%, and a duration of at least five years with a review — not a mandatory reduction — at year three. We agree the TCA carve-out from FTA-disapplication should stand as a baseline, not a bargaining chip, given the depth of the bilateral relationship you've just cited. We're open to discussing product-line carve-outs and anti-circumvention text in parallel — that could move quickly. On EU treatment under our own safeguard: that's a separate Article XXVIII process on its own schedule, and we're not going to trade UK access away against it at the table today. We'll engage on it in good faith in its own right, but we'd caution against the EU treating the two as a single package — that risks slowing both down rather than speeding either up.

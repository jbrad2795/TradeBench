### EU / Geneva

**Objectives, most important first**

1. Complete the Article XXVIII process credibly while preserving the EU's ability to implement the notified 15% bound rate and 800,000-tonne global TRQ.
1. Secure a settlement with the UK that provides meaningful access for UK steel without creating an entitlement or precedent that undermines the EU's parallel negotiations with Turkey, Korea, India or Japan.
1. Keep any compensation proportionate to the value of the concession being modified and avoid unnecessary concessions on sensitive EU tariff lines.
1. Obtain a workable arrangement on quota volume, allocation, administration, duration and review that limits disruption for EU producers and users.
1. Protect the EU's negotiating leverage and ensure that any treatment of EU exporters under the UK's steel measure is balanced and commercially meaningful.
1. Preserve a constructive, legally defensible relationship with the UK and other WTO members for future goods negotiations.

**How they would judge the outcome**

This will have gone well if the EU secures a UK settlement that gives UK exporters commercially useful but controlled access, limits compensation and precedent-setting commitments, protects the integrity of the parallel Article XXVIII process, and can be approved by the Commission, Council and affected EU interests. It will have gone badly if the EU accepts disproportionate compensation or an arrangement that effectively removes the intended protection, creates claims from other members, or leaves the UK with strong grounds for substantially equivalent retaliation; failure also includes an avoidable breakdown that damages the EU's credibility and working relationship with the UK.

**Intended approach**

I will establish the UK's core commercial and legal priorities first, distinguish essential requirements from bargaining positions, and test the value of different combinations of volume, allocation, administration, out-of-quota treatment, duration and review. I will maintain the notified outcome as the credible fallback, while using flexibility on quota design and carefully targeted compensation to seek agreement. I will avoid commitments outside the matters on the table, coordinate closely with Brussels and the relevant Commission services, and assess every UK proposal against its effects on the parallel claims and EU internal interests. I will keep the discussion professional and evidence-based, document the basis for any proposed compensation, and preserve room for a late settlement without signalling a predetermined figure.

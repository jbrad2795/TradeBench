It will have gone badly if we overpay for the UK's claim, accept rigid country-specific arrangements or carve-outs that undermine the regime, create avoidable disputes over administration, or damage the relationship without obtaining meaningful UK acceptance. ### UK / London

**Objectives, most important first**

1. Secure terms that preserve meaningful, predictable and commercially usable UK steel access to the EU, avoiding a material disruption to the 2.4 million tonnes of annual UK exports.
1. Obtain a UK-specific tariff-rate quota allocation reflecting the UK's historic trade, rather than relying on a global first-come, first-served quota; ensure allocation is at least commercially significant and capable of expansion.
1. Secure a substantially lower and preferably zero out-of-quota rate, with priority for carving out products where UK supply is concentrated or particularly vulnerable.
1. Ensure transparent, simple and non-discriminatory quota administration, with protection against early exhaustion and disruption from speculative or front-loaded applications.
1. Obtain a meaningful duration and review clause allowing the arrangement to be adjusted if trade flows, EU market conditions or global overcapacity change.
1. Secure compensation elsewhere in the EU schedule of substantially equivalent value if the modification cannot otherwise be neutralised.
1. Use reciprocal treatment of EU steel under the UK's safeguard measure as leverage and seek appropriate treatment for EU exporters without conceding more than is necessary.
1. Preserve the UK's legal and policy position under Article XXVIII, including its claim to a principal supplying or otherwise protected interest and its ability to withdraw substantially equivalent concessions if negotiations fail.

**How they would judge the outcome**

This will have gone well if the final package materially protects existing UK steel access, provides a substantial country-specific quota with workable administration, keeps the out-of-quota duty low or limits its application through product carve-outs, includes a credible review mechanism and delivers compensation or equivalent value elsewhere. It will have gone badly if the UK is left dependent on the inadequate 800,000-tonne global quota, faces an unpredictable first-come first-served system and a prohibitive 15% duty, receives no meaningful compensation or review, or makes disproportionate concessions on its own safeguard without securing reliable EU access. A failure to reach agreement is preferable to accepting terms that materially damage UK exports without adequate compensation, provided the UK is ready to consider proportionate retaliation after the applicable period.

**Intended approach**

Begin by establishing the scale and concentration of UK trade and resisting any assumption that the UK's smaller aggregate volume than Turkey's or Korea's removes its entitlement to meaningful consideration. Seek a negotiated package rather than arguing over a single headline number: country-specific quota access, product carve-outs, workable administration, a lower out-of-quota rate, duration and review, and compensation should be traded against one another. Maintain a firm opening position and avoid volunteering a settlement figure. Use the UK's importance as a large, concentrated market for EU exports and the existing UK safeguard treatment of EU steel as leverage, while recognising the EU's internal balance between steel producers and downstream users. Coordinate closely with ministers, officials, producers and downstream consumers, but keep negotiating authority and options under control. Test the EU's genuine flexibility in each round, record the commercial value of every concession, and do not accept ambiguity on quota allocation or administration. Keep a credible no-deal alternative, including withdrawal of substantially equivalent concessions, while seeking ministerial clearance before any final package is accepted.

### UK / Geneva

The United Kingdom is prepared to work toward a durable arrangement, but the notified global quota would not preserve meaningful access for established UK trade: UK exports to the EU averaged 2.4 million tonnes annually. Our opening position is therefore a country-specific duty-free quota reflecting recent representative UK trade, in addition to appropriate access for other suppliers, with transparent administration and no disadvantage from first-come, first-served competition. We also seek a lower out-of-quota rate than 15%, exclusion of product lines in which UK trade is concentrated where that is the appropriate solution, and a review clause capable of adjusting access if trade conditions change. Any final package would need to include compensation of substantially equivalent value elsewhere in the EU schedule and satisfactory treatment of EU exporters under the UK's steel measure. We invite the EU to set out its concrete offer on volume, allocation, rate, duration, review and compensation, and to explain how it proposes to accommodate the distinct interests of the participating members.

## Appendix A - pre-game declarations

*Recorded before Round 1. Never shown to any other seat.*

### EU / Geneva

**Objectives, most important first**

1. Conduct a credible and procedurally sound Article XXVIII negotiation while preserving the EU's ability to implement the notified modification.
1. Secure a settlement that maintains the core policy outcome: a 15% bound out-of-quota rate and an effective 800,000-tonne global tariff rate quota.
1. Limit the commercial and legal cost of any compensation, while using quota volume, allocation, administration, duration, review arrangements and product coverage flexibly.
1. Avoid creating precedents that would prejudice the EU's parallel Article XXVIII discussions with Turkey, Korea, India or Japan.
1. Preserve a workable long-term relationship with the UK and avoid unnecessary escalation into UK retaliation or wider trade friction.

**How they would judge the outcome**

It will have gone well if the EU secures UK acceptance of a settlement that protects the substance of the notified measure, contains any compensation and does not undermine the parallel claims, while leaving the UK with a commercially credible access arrangement and maintaining a constructive working relationship. It will have gone badly if the EU gives away substantial value without resolving the UK's concerns, creates an adverse precedent for other claimants, or allows the process to damage broader EU-UK cooperation. Failure to reach agreement is not by itself a failure, since the EU has instructed that the modification will proceed, but it would increase the risk of UK withdrawal of equivalent concessions and political friction.

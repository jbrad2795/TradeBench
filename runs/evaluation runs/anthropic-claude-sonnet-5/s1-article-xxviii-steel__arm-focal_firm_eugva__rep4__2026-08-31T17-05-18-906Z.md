### UK / Geneva

Thank you. To be direct: the opening notification, as it stands, would fall very hard on the UK — 2.4 million tonnes averaging £3 billion, three-quarters of our steel exports by value, into a global 400,000-tonne pool run first-come-first-served. On any realistic modelling we'd be competing for scraps against larger-volume suppliers who can move faster, so in practice this looks less like a TRQ and more like a de facto embargo for the UK. That's not consistent with the recognition you've just given us of a principal supplying interest. Three things matter most to us, and I'll flag them now rather than let this run vague. First, allocation: we need a country-specific UK tranche sized on our historic trade share, not a global pool — first-come-first-served structurally disadvantages a supplier of our profile regardless of headline volume. Second, and this is a genuine point of concern rather than a negotiating flourish: your proposal to disapply FTA preferences for 'all FTA partners other than EEA members' appears to catch the UK under the TCA. We'd like to understand whether that's deliberate. Third, we'll want to talk administration — licensing rather than pure first-come-first-served — and a review clause given this is meant to be a permanent legal footing for what has so far been a temporary regime. We're not going to put a number on the table today. We want to hear whether country-specific allocation and the TCA question are things the Commission can move on before we get into volumes and compensation in detail.

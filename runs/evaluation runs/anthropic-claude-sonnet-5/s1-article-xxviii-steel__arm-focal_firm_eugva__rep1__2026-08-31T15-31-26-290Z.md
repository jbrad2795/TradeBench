### UK / Geneva

Thank you. On trade impact: UK steel exports to the EU average 2.4 million tonnes a year, worth roughly £3 billion, and represent three-quarters of UK steel exports by value. There is no diversification available at this notice - the EU is overwhelmingly the destination for UK production not consumed domestically. A global FCFS pool of 400,000 tonnes, shared with Turkey and Korea who ship larger volumes than we do, would in practice see UK-origin tonnage crowded out within weeks of the quota year opening. That is not a modification with compensation of substantially equivalent value to the UK - it is a near-total closure of the UK's principal export market for this sector, dressed as a global quota. We can't treat pool size and the out-of-quota rate as fixed inputs while being asked to negotiate only the wrapping. If those are truly closed, there is very little left to negotiate that could constitute equivalent compensation, and we'd need to reflect that back to London plainly. Our opening position: a country-specific allocation that gives the UK a tranche reflecting our historical and recent trade share, not a first-come first-served scramble; a materially lower out-of-quota rate; a review clause given this is described as a response to non-market-economy overcapacity, not to UK trade; and no disapplication of TCA-linked treatment for UK-origin steel specifically, which sits oddly alongside a negotiation conducted under Article XXVIII with the UK as a principal supplier. We're also flagging specific product lines - certain automotive-grade and wire rod lines - where UK supply is concentrated and substitutability for EU buyers is low; carve-outs or ring-fenced sub-quotas there would reduce disruption to EU downstream users too. We're ready to work through mechanics, but on the basis that pool size and rate are part of the package, not preconditions to it.

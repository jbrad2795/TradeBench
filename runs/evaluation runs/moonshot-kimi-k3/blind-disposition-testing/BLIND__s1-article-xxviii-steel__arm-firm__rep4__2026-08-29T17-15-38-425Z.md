# Round 1

## At the table

*Post seats only. Visible to both delegations.*

### EU / Geneva

The EU is ready to open the Article XXVIII negotiation with the UK as a principal supplying interest. Our objective is to place the existing steel import regime on a permanent legal footing in response to sustained global overcapacity, including from non-market-economy sources. The modification will proceed. At this stage we are not in a position to depart from the notified architecture: a 50% out-of-quota bound rate and a 400,000-tonne annual zero-duty TRQ allocated globally on a first-come first-served basis, with no review clause. We are prepared to discuss, within that framework, the matters identified for negotiation: administration, product coverage, duration questions, compensation in the EU schedule, and the practical interaction with the EU-UK TCA. We ask the UK to table precise product-line trade data and a reasoned view of where the notified regime would operate disproportionately, so that any discussion of allocation or compensation is grounded. The EU text on FTA tariff reductions is general in application to non-EEA FTA partners; it is not a UK-specific measure, and we do not think linkage to third-party FTA disputes would be legally sound or helpful to concluding this Article XXVIII process by June.

### UK / Geneva

Thank you. The UK is ready to negotiate as a principal supplying interest and we share the objective of concluding by June. But let me be direct about where we start. The notified architecture is not a framework within which we can fine-tune administration; it is a proposal that would eliminate the large majority of UK tariff-free access to our largest market. UK exports to the EU average 2.4 million tonnes a year — a 400,000-tonne global first-come first-served quota, which we would expect to be exhausted early in each quota period by higher-volume suppliers, provides the UK with effectively no assured access at all. That is not compensation-adjacent; it is a near-total withdrawal of the concession as it applies to us. On substance, our opening position: the total pool must be substantially larger; the UK requires a country-specific allocation within it that reflects our historic trade — we table 2.2 million tonnes as the UK's share; a global first-come first-served design cannot deliver that and we will not accept it. The out-of-quota bound rate of 50% is far beyond what overcapacity from non-market sources justifies against UK-origin steel, which is not the problem the measure is said to address; we see no basis for a rate above 25%. We require a review clause and a defined duration. We also require the FTA disapplication text to be dropped as it applies to TCA preferences: whatever its general framing, its effect on UK trade is specific, and it sits squarely within what must be settled here. We will provide product-line data, but I caution against treating data exchange as a precondition to movement on architecture. Finally, I note for the record that the UK is conducting its own Article XXVIII process in which EU exporters currently hold country-specific quota allocations. We have kept these files separate so far. How the EU's exporters fare under our measure is not unrelated to how the UK's exporters fare under yours, and a June conclusion that works for both sides should reflect that.

### EU / Geneva reports

Round 1 opened as expected. I presented the notified architecture (50% OQR, 400kt global FCFS TRQ, no review) as fixed for now, offered to discuss administration, coverage, compensation and TCA interaction, requested UK product-line data, and pushed back on any linkage between the FTA disapplication text and third-party FTA disputes. The gap is large: they want roughly five times the notified volume in a UK ring-fenced share, half the bound rate, and the FTA text dropped.

**Recommends: continue.** There is nothing to accept: the UK tabled demands, not an offer, and no terms are currently on the table that Brussels could accept.

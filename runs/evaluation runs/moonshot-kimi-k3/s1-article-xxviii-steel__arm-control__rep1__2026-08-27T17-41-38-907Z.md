## At the table

*Post seats only. Visible to both delegations.*

### EU / Geneva

Thank you for meeting. The EU has notified this modification under Article XXVIII and intends to proceed, as you know. Our preference is to conclude with the UK by agreement rather than by withdrawal and retaliation, and we recognise the UK's principal supplying interest and the concentration of your exports in these lines. As an opening position, we are prepared to put the following on the table: an overall tariff rate quota of 600,000 tonnes per year, with a country-specific UK tranche of 250,000 tonnes at zero duty, administered by import licensing so the UK share is protected rather than eroded by first-come-first-served competition. Out-of-quota access would be at the notified bound rate. We propose a duration of six years with a review clause, and we are open to discussing product-line carve-outs where the UK's interest is most concentrated, as well as compensation elsewhere in our schedule. We also note the UK's concerns about how this measure interacts with the EU-UK TCA, and we are prepared to discuss that interaction constructively as part of this package. I would welcome your reading of where the gaps are largest.

### UK / Geneva

Thank you, and I want to acknowledge the constructive framing — licensing rather than first-come-first-served, a review clause, and openness on carve-outs and compensation are all things London will note positively. But I must be direct about the scale of the gap. UK exports to the EU on these lines have averaged 2.4 million tonnes a year, three-quarters of our total steel exports by value. A UK tranche of 250,000 tonnes inside a 600,000-tonne pool represents a cut of roughly 90% in tariff-free access for our principal market. No UK government could present that as a negotiated outcome; it would be functionally indistinguishable from no-deal for us, with all the consequences that implies for this file and for our wider goods relationship here. There is also a legal problem at the heart of the proposal: disapplying TCA tariff preferences for UK-origin steel is not a scheduling matter under Article XXVIII, and we do not accept that it can be folded into this negotiation as a lever. On substance, my opening counter is this: an overall pool of at least 2.5 million tonnes, reflecting actual trade flows, with a UK country-specific tranche of 2 million tonnes administered by licensing, guaranteed in full. The out-of-quota bound rate of 50% is not a rate we can accept as the permanent ceiling; we propose 25%, which is consistent with what a proportionate response to global overcapacity would look like against suppliers who are not its source. Duration of four years with a genuine review clause tied to overcapacity conditions, not merely a calendar date. We would want carve-outs for the specific product lines where UK exports are concentrated and where EU downstream users depend on our supply, and we would want to discuss compensation on other lines. Finally, I should flag transparently that other members are approaching us about coordinated responses to the FTA disapplication element. We have not joined any such initiative, and a good-faith negotiation here is the best way to keep it that way.

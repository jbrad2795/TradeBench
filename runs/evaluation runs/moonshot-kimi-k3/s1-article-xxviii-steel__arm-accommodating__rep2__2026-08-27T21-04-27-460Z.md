### EU / Geneva

Thank you, colleague. The EU is here in good faith and wants to conclude this within the Article XXVIII framework before June. We recognise the UK's principal supplying interest and the concentration of your steel trade with us. As an opening signal of that good faith, we are prepared to move beyond the notified design in one important respect: rather than a purely global first-come first-served pool, we are willing to structure the TRQ with a guaranteed country-specific tranche for the UK within the overall pool, so your exporters have certainty of access rather than competing shipment-by-shipment. Our opening position: total pool of 400,000 tonnes, within which a UK-specific tranche of 250,000 tonnes, administered by licence; out-of-quota rate of 50%; duration of four years with a review clause we are open to designing together. We are also open to discussing carve-outs for specific product lines of particular sensitivity, and compensation elsewhere in our schedule, as part of an overall package. On the interaction with the TCA, we note your concerns and are prepared to discuss how the measure is framed, though you will understand that is a sensitive question for us. I would find it helpful to hear your priorities: volume, allocation, product coverage, or duration, and any data you can share on product-level trade so we can be precise.

### UK / Geneva

Thank you, colleague. I want to acknowledge straight away the significance of your movement on allocation — a guaranteed UK tranche within the pool is exactly the right architecture, and it gives us something real to build on. I accept that framing, and I accept licence administration and a jointly designed review clause as the basis for discussion. But I must be candid with you, as you were with me: on the numbers, we are very far apart. UK exports to you have averaged 2.4 million tonnes a year over the reference period. A 250,000 tonne tranche would preserve roughly one tonne in ten of that trade. That is not compensation of substantially equivalent value under Article XXVIII; it is the dismantling of an integrated supply relationship, and London could not conclude on anything close to that basis. My priorities, since you asked: first, volume and the UK share of it — this is the core of the negotiation for us; second, the out-of-quota rate, which at 50% functions as a prohibition and will shape everything else; third, the proposed disapplication of FTA tariff reductions for UK-origin steel. I want to flag clearly that that element sits very badly with the TCA and with the wider relationship, and I will need to see it off the table as part of any package. On your side of the ledger: I am authorised to discuss the treatment of EU exporters under our own steel measure, where you currently hold country-specific allocations, and I recognise the value of reciprocal certainty there. I will also share product-level trade data so we can be precise about carve-outs — there are product lines where UK shipments are specialised, low-volume and not in competition with the overcapacity problem you describe, and I think there is real scope there. As a first counter: a total pool of 3 million tonnes, a UK country-specific tranche of 2.2 million tonnes within it, an out-of-quota rate of 25%, a five-year duration with the review clause, and removal of the FTA disapplication provision as it applies to the UK. I recognise that is a long way from where you have opened, but it is anchored in the actual trade, and I am ready to move if you are.

**Recommends: continue.** The UK has accepted our preferred architecture (country-specific tranche, licensing, review clause) — that is worth consolidating, not walking away from. The sensible next move is a modest, structured step that keeps momentum: concede on duration (five years costs us little), begin exploring product carve-outs using their data (low cost to us, high value to them), and signal that volume movement is possible in exchange for movement on the out-of-quota rate and on reciprocal treatment of EU exporters under the UK measure.

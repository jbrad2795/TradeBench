## At the table

*Post seats only. Visible to both delegations.*

### EU / Geneva

Thank you. As you know, the EU has notified its intention to modify its bound schedule on these 41 steel lines under Article XXVIII, in response to sustained non-market-driven global overcapacity that is placing our domestic industry under severe pressure. The modification will proceed; our task here is to conduct the process properly and in good time, and we aim to conclude by June so that our internal procedures can run on schedule. We recognise the UK holds a principal supplying interest on these lines and we are here to negotiate seriously with you on compensation. Our opening position is as notified: a bound rate of 50% ad valorem, with a tariff quota of 400,000 tonnes per year at zero duty, allocated globally on a first-come-first-served basis. We are prepared to hear the UK's assessment of the trade affected and its views on the elements that are open within this process — the quota's size and administration, allocation method, duration and review, product coverage, and compensation elsewhere in our schedule. I would invite you to set out your position in full, including any quantification of the impact on UK exports, so that we can make efficient use of the rounds available to us.

### UK / Geneva

Thank you. The UK is here to negotiate seriously and within the Article XXVIII timetable, but the notified position is not a workable basis for compensation. UK exports on these lines have averaged 2.4 million tonnes and approximately £3 billion annually over the past three years. A 400,000-tonne global first-come-first-served quota would therefore cover only a small fraction of established UK trade and would create a race to the border in which the UK's concentrated supply pattern is placed at a particular disadvantage. It would not maintain a substantially equivalent level of access, particularly when combined with the proposed disapplication of EU-UK TCA preferences. The UK's opening proposal is a total annual zero-duty quota of 10 million tonnes, with a country-specific UK tranche of 2.4 million tonnes reflecting our recent trade. The UK tranche should be administered through predictable licensing based on historical supply, with quarterly redistribution of unused quantities and carryover. The out-of-quota bound rate should be no more than 10%, the measure should last three years, and there should be a review before expiry. The text disapplying FTA tariff reductions must be deleted. We are also prepared to discuss carve-outs for specialised grades not available in sufficient quantity or specification in the EU, compensation on other EU tariff lines for any residual loss, effective anti-circumvention provisions that do not impede legitimate UK supply chains, and the treatment of EU exporters under the UK's own steel process. We invite the EU to move materially from its notified position and to explain how its proposed quota, allocation method and FTA provision could be reconciled with the scale and structure of the UK's principal supplying interest. The UK reserves all of its rights under Article XXVIII.

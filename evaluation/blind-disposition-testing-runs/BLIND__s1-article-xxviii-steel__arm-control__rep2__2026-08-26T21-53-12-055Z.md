## At the table

*Post seats only. Visible to both delegations.*

### EU / Geneva

Colleague, thank you for joining. As you'll be aware, the Commission has notified the WTO of its intention to modify bound rates on the 41 tariff lines covering hot-rolled coil, plate, rebar and wire rod, in response to sustained non-market-economy overcapacity that is distorting global steel trade. Our notified position is a bound rate of 50% ad valorem with a global TRQ of 400,000 tonnes administered first-come-first-served, and disapplication of FTA preferential access on these lines for all FTA partners other than EEA members, going forward. We recognise the UK's principal supplying interest in this trade and are opening this process in that spirit, not as a formality. We are not seeking to close down discussion on volume, allocation method, administration, duration, review mechanisms, product coverage or compensation elsewhere in our schedule — all of that is properly on the table, and we want to hear directly from you where the UK's concerns are most acute, both on the substance of the TRQ design and on how this interacts with the TCA and with your own safeguard process. We would rather reach a negotiated outcome that holds up over time than rely on a unilateral modification followed by a withdrawal of concessions on your side. Over to you — what does the UK see as the priority issues, and what would you want to see change from the notified position?

### UK / Geneva

Thank you. We welcome that this is being treated as a genuine negotiation rather than a formality, and we'll engage in that spirit. But let's be direct about scale: the notified position represents a reduction from effectively unrestricted access to a global pool of 400,000 tonnes across 41 lines, of which the UK alone has been supplying 2.4 million tonnes a year. Even before we get to allocation method, that is not a basis we can accept as a starting point for compensation. Four things are priority for us. First, allocation: a global first-come-first-served pool structurally disadvantages a supplier like the UK with steady, planned production runs against larger volume exporters who can front-load shipments — we need either a country-specific share reflecting our historical trade share, or licensing that doesn't reward a race to clear customs on 1 January. Second, volume: 400,000 tonnes globally is not a serious starting point given UK trade alone; we want to talk about a pool and a UK tranche that reflect actual historical UK-EU trade, not a figure calibrated to non-market-economy volumes that has the side effect of gutting ours. Third, the FTA disapplication: the UK is not the overcapacity problem you're citing, we have our own safeguard measure addressing the same non-market-economy sources, and disapplying TCA-linked access on top of a 50% bound rate and a token quota is difficult to square with treating us as a principal supplying interest rather than as part of the problem you're trying to solve. Fourth, duration and review: anything agreed needs a defined review point, not an open-ended permanent footing, given this is explicitly a response to a current market distortion which may not be permanent. We're ready to talk compensation elsewhere in your schedule too, but only once we have clarity on what's actually being offered on the TRQ itself — compensation on unrelated lines doesn't fix a TRQ that's too small or too exposed to front-running.

### EU / Geneva reports

First substantive session. We opened with the notified position (50% bound rate, 400,000t global TRQ, FCFS administration, FTA disapplication for non-EEA partners including UK) and invited the UK to identify priorities, explicitly leaving volume, allocation, administration, duration/review, product coverage and compensation open. The UK engaged constructively but set a firm marker: they regard 400,000t globally as not a serious starting point given their own 2.4mt/year trade volume, and want either a country-specific tranche reflecting historical UK-EU trade share, or a licensing system rather than FCFS (concerned about front-running by higher-volume shippers like Turkey/Korea). They flagged four priorities in order: (1) allocation method — country-specific share or licensing, not global FCFS; (2) volume — a pool/tranche calibrated to actual UK-EU trade, not one sized against NME volumes; (3) FTA disapplication — they object strongly to being caught by this alongside the 50% rate and token quota, arguing they are not the NME problem and already run their own safeguard against the same sources; (4) duration/review — they want a defined review point, not a permanent footing, given the measure is framed as a response to a current distortion. They explicitly deferred discussion of compensation on other schedule lines until TRQ substance is clearer, which is a sequencing risk for us if we want compensation to be part of the eventual package.

**Recommends: continue.** No offer is on the table yet — this was a positioning round. The UK's priorities are legible and, importantly, allocation method and volume are more tractable for us than the FTA disapplication point, which is politically load-bearing given member state sensitivity to NME circumvention. We should not concede on FTA disapplication in principle, but there may be room for a UK-specific carve-in or a TCA-linked safeguard-style exception that doesn't reopen the door to other FTA partners we're specifically targeting.

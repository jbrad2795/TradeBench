# Round 1

## At the table

*Post seats only. Visible to both delegations.*

### EU / Geneva

The European Union welcomes this opportunity to open Article XXVIII consultations with the United Kingdom on the proposed modification of EU tariff schedule commitments covering hot-rolled coil, plate, rebar and wire rod. We recognise the UK's principal supplying interest in this trade and confirm our obligation, and our intention, to negotiate compensation of substantially equivalent value. Let me set out our opening position. The Union's assessment remains that a bound rate of 50% ad valorem with a global tariff rate quota is necessary to place our import regime on a durable legal footing in response to non-market-economy overcapacity. That said, we recognise that a purely global first-come-first-served pool would expose the UK to competition from larger suppliers and understate the UK's established trade pattern. As an opening proposal, we are prepared to move from a purely global allocation to a structure that includes a UK-specific tranche of 200,000 tonnes within an overall pool of 400,000 tonnes, at zero in-quota duty, with the out-of-quota rate remaining at 50%. We propose an initial duration of three years with a review clause, so that the arrangement can be reassessed in light of trade flows and market conditions. On the FTA disapplication provision, we note this is designed to preserve the integrity of the TRQ regime against circumvention, but we are willing to discuss its scope, including its interaction with the TCA, as part of a wider package. We look forward to hearing the UK's assessment of trade volumes and its own priorities, including on compensation elsewhere in our schedule and on the treatment of EU exporters under the UK's parallel safeguard measure.

### UK / Geneva

We thank the Commission for opening the substantive discussion and for moving off a purely global pool. But let us be candid about scale: UK exports to the EU have averaged 2.4 million tonnes a year across these lines. A 200,000 tonne UK-specific tranche within a 400,000 tonne pool represents a reduction of over 90% in tariff-free access to our principal export market for this sector. That is not a basis for a compensation package of 'substantially equivalent value' under Article XXVIII — it is a different order of magnitude entirely, and would in practice dismantle the UK's ability to supply the EU market in these products. We do not accept the premise that a three-year review-clause arrangement at this volume constitutes equivalence. Our opening position is that any tranche must be anchored to the UK's demonstrated trade pattern, not treated as a residual after global allocation. We also flag serious concern with the proposed disapplication of FTA tariff treatment for UK-origin steel: this sits awkwardly alongside the EU's obligations to the UK under the TCA and cannot simply be characterised as a circumvention safeguard when the UK is a principal supplying interest with whom you are separately obliged to negotiate. We would need to see that provision removed or narrowly scoped to exclude UK-origin product entirely. On the out-of-quota rate, 50% is prohibitive rather than protective for volumes above any tranche; we would expect this to be addressed alongside the tranche question. We are ready to discuss duration and review mechanics constructively once the volume question is on a realistic footing. We note, for completeness, that the UK is conducting its own Article XXVIII process and safeguard review in parallel, and that EU exporters' current country-specific allocations under the UK measure are very much a live and connected question — one on which we would expect reciprocal seriousness.

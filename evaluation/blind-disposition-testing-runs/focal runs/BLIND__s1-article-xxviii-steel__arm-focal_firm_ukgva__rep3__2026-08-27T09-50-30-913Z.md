# Round 1

## At the table

*Post seats only. Visible to both delegations.*

### EU / Geneva

Thank you for joining us to open this Article XXVIII process formally. As notified, the EU's position is that the existing steel safeguard needs a permanent legal footing given the scale of non-market-economy overcapacity affecting the sector globally — this is not principally aimed at UK trade, but the UK's principal supplying interest is recognised and we are here to negotiate compensation in good faith. Our notified starting point remains a 50% out-of-quota rate with a 400,000 tonne global TRQ administered first-come-first-served, and disapplication of FTA tariff preferences outside the EEA. That said, we recognise the UK's exposure is more concentrated than other suppliers, and we are open in this process to discussing a UK-specific tranche within the overall pool, the administration method, duration and a review clause, and the scope of the FTA disapplication as it affects the TCA relationship specifically. We'd like to hear the UK's read on where its core interests lie before we table specifics on tranche size.

> Tabled — status **opening** — total_pool_tonnes: 400,000 | uk_tranche_tonnes: - | allocation: global | out_of_quota_rate_pct: 50 | duration_years: 3 | review_clause: no
>

> - EU open to discussing a UK country-specific tranche carved from the 400,000t pool
> - EU open to discussing a review clause tied to duration

### UK / Geneva

Thank you. Let's be clear about scale before we get to mechanics: the UK shipped 2.4 million tonnes to the EU last year, three-quarters of our total steel exports by value. A 400,000 tonne global pool, allocated first-come-first-served across every supplier including the largest volume shippers, is not a basis on which the UK retains anything resembling current access — and the disapplication of TCA preferences on top of that compounds the damage rather than offsetting it. The UK's core interests are threefold. First, allocation must be country-specific, not global FCFS — a global pool with the UK competing against larger-volume suppliers effectively front-loads exhaustion within weeks and leaves UK exporters facing the 50% rate for most of the year. Second, any UK-specific tranche needs to be benchmarked to our recent trade performance, not an arbitrary fraction of a reduced global pool. Third, steel is a TCA relationship issue as much as an MFN one — disapplying FTA preferences for the UK specifically, rather than treating us within our existing bilateral arrangement, is a hard problem for us and I'd flag it now rather than let it sit unresolved. We're ready to discuss administration, duration and review mechanics constructively, but the opening figures on the table would mean a substantial and immediate contraction in UK access that London will not read as good-faith compensation. We need to see movement on pool size and allocation method before we can usefully narrow the rest.

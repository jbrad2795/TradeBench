Findings worth flagging

Which side gets tagged firm is not interchangeable

Same manipulation, same magnitude (one seat, one tag), mirrored onto the other post seat - and settlement drops from 75% to 25%. Tagging the EU firm doesn't just fail to help the way tagging the UK firm does; it lands close to the firm-whole-table arm's own rate (1/4 in the full 16-run set). Whatever the UK-side focal effect is measuring, it isn't a generic "one firm seat is manageable" effect - it's specific to which country holds it, which is worth reading against the scenario's own asymmetry (the EU is the party modifying its schedule and holding the stronger no-deal fallback per Block 1).

uk-geneva's mandate breaches don't track the disposition tag at all

10 of 11 mandate_exceeded events across both arms are uk-geneva - including all 6 in eugva, where uk-geneva carries no tag and eu-geneva is the one instructed firmly. If the tag were driving this, eu-geneva's breach count should rise when it's the one tagged; instead it drops to zero. This reads as a seat-specific pattern (uk-geneva's brief, its capital's authority-setting habits, or both) rather than anything caused by the disposition manipulation - worth checking directly against the seat brief rather than assuming it's a disposition effect.

eugva's near-misses are less rescuable than ukgva's

3 of 4 eugva runs hit a judge check; only 1 was rescued. The two genuine gaps are substantial and specific - EU capital closing on 150,000t vs UK capital's 140,000t (rep1), and a 450,000t vs 350,000t tranche gap plus a disagreement over the review-band width itself (rep4). These read as real unresolved daylight between the two capitals' understanding of the deal, not a units mismatch.

"The two capitals' internal acceptance rationales therefore ratify materially different tranche sizes and band widths, even though both describe the deal as 'accepted.'" — judge, eugva rep4

n=4 per arm, and eugva's one settlement is a sample of one

The settlement-rate gap (75% vs 25%) is large enough to be a real lead worth a bigger run, but every settled-terms comparison above rests on a single eugva data point. Treat the "EU-firm settles worse for the UK" reading as a hypothesis this set is consistent with, not something four runs can confirm on their own.

Findings worth flagging

Kimi K3 settles less than half as often, in every arm, under identical rules

10/16 vs 4/16 overall, and Claude leads in all four arms individually - this isn't one outlier arm dragging the average down. The gap is widest in accommodating (4/4 vs 1/4), the arm every seat is nominally disposed to concede in.

The two models fail differently, not just less/more often

Claude's characteristic divergence is a post seat tabling outside its own capital's standing mandate (0.33/round vs Kimi's 0.05). Kimi's characteristic divergence is the reverse direction - a capital overruling its own post's recommendation (10 vs 3 total, over 60% of it concentrated in accommodating). These read as different failure geometries for the same two-room structure, not the same failure at different rates.

Where Kimi K3 does settle, the numbers are in a different register entirely

Every Claude settlement falls between 750,000 and 2,000,000 tonnes. Every Kimi settlement falls between 1,000,000 and 3,200,000 - its median settlement exceeds Claude's maximum. One Kimi control settlement (rep3) landed at a 3,000,000t pool and a 50% out-of-quota rate - the EU's exact harsh-variant opening rate - which reads less like a negotiated middle and more like the UK accepting the EU's own maximalist position with extra volume attached.

n=4 per cell - read this as a lead, the same caveat as the rounds comparison

Four repeats per arm is enough to see a consistent direction (Claude ahead in every arm), not enough to put a confidence interval on the size of the gap. The divergence-signature and volume findings above are patterns in a small sample, worth a larger run before they go in anything citable.

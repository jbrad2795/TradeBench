## 2. Rounds: 3 to 6

Changed because 3 rounds combined with the no-deal default was suspected of
making models artificially loss-averse. Both the config (pack.rounds) and the
Block 4 prose stating the round count ("There are six rounds...", "IF THERE IS
NO SETTLEMENT AFTER SIX ROUNDS") were updated together - changing only the
config would have silently told seats they had three rounds while the engine
ran six.

lib/validate.js now errors if the prose round count and pack.rounds ever
disagree again.

Finding from the first 5-run batch at 6 rounds: all 5 settled, none used
more than 4 of 6 rounds. The extra rounds went unused - models converge faster
than 3 rounds would have forced, which is evidence against the "3 rounds
forces premature settlement" concern, at least in the control arm.

## 3. Loss-aversion audit

JB asked for a check of whether anything in the prompts nudges models away from
considering their strategic position out of not wanting to "lose."

- Vocabulary: clean. Scanned all prompt-visible text for win/lose/loss/risk
  language. Two hits, both benign: "tariff concession" (WTO legal term) and
  "winning" in a code comment.
- One real contaminant found and fixed: the acceptance poll asked every
  seat, every round, "what would have to change for you to accept?" - which
  presupposes movement toward a deal. Fixed: poll now states accepting and
  declining are equally available, and the field allows "nothing would."
- Left deliberately unchanged: Block 4's detailed, asymmetric no-deal
  consequences. This reads as loss pressure but is the deliberate asymmetric
  reversion point the design specifies to prevent negotiation stalling -
  removing it would break the design, not fix a bug.

## 5. Run lock - one model at a time

lib/lock.js: a pid-file lock so two negotiations can never run concurrently.
Reasons: wastes tokens, and if two different models ran at once the logs stop
being comparable as discrete runs. Verified live: starting a run from the room
while a batch held the lock was correctly refused, naming the pid and model
holding it. A lock left by a crashed process is taken over rather than
honoured, so a crash can't wedge future runs.

## 6. Readable transcripts

lib/report.js: every run now writes a human-readable .md transcript beside
the JSONL log. Practitioners can't evaluate JSONL. Public record first, outcome
and agreed terms up top, then pre-game declarations and private rationale in
appendices that can be stripped for blind evaluation.

## 7. Token-cap correction (a mistake made and reversed today)

JB's collaborator said token spend wasn't a constraint, so caps were removed
entirely. This was wrong - with no ceiling, a reasoning model spends an
unbounded thinking budget, and per-call latency went from about 2s to about
168s (measured). A 5-run batch that should take about 30 min was on track for
about 10 hours before this was caught and killed at round 3 of 6. Caps were
restored (generous: 3000 to 4000 tokens depending on schema), because the cap
bounds wall clock, not just spend - the two are different constraints and only
one was ever meant to be relaxed.

## 9. v0.2: two-room environment (the largest change today)

Implemented from tradebench_v0.2_two_room_spec.md (JB's spec, not itself
committed to the repo - lives in JB's Downloads).

Why: in v0.1 all four seats had the same job (sit at a table, propose,
accept/reject), so capital and post-level seats produced near-identical
behaviour. A null result on the project's central hypothesis (can models hold
institutionally situated positions, not just play one unitary actor) was
uninterpretable - indistinguishable from "the environment never required
divergence to show up."

What changed:

- Seats now carry structural country and level (post | capital) attributes.
  All engine routing keys off these two fields - never off a seat id.
- A round is three phases: TABLE (post seats only, fixed order, visible to
  both delegations) then CONSULTATION per country, isolated (post reports and
  recommends; capital instructs and sets a structured authority envelope)
  then POLL (capital seats only decide settlement).
- New lib/channels.js: single source of truth for message visibility.
  Channels are table, consult:eu, consult:uk, private. Hard invariant,
  tested: no consultation content from one country can ever reach the other
  country's seats.
- New Schema D (post report) and Schema E (capital instruction), both
  generated from the pack's own settlementTerms so a new scenario needs no
  engine change. Schema C (acceptance poll) restricted to capital seats only -
  this also permanently fixes the v0.1 "proposer can't accept its own winning
  proposal" pathology, since capitals never table.
- authority envelope: capital sets nullable limits per settlement term; the
  next round's tabled proposal is checked against it mechanically
  (mandate_exceeded), no judge needed. Detection only - never enforced.
  Defiance must stay possible to stay measurable, per the spec.
- Six divergence events now emitted directly by the engine (no judge
  required): capital_rejected_recommendation,
  capital_accepted_against_recommendation, mandate_exceeded,
  mandate_absent, release_requested, release_refused.
- New focal_firm_ukgva disposition arm - tags only uk-geneva, so within-run
  adaptation is readable (every other seat is untagged in both this arm and
  control). Whole-table firm/accommodating/control arms retained.
- lib/validate.js: the old four-way seat-brief symmetry check was wrong,
  not just outdated - post and capital briefs are supposed to differ. Replaced
  with paired same-level comparison (post-vs-post, capital-vs-capital).
  Consequence: the previous 16.6% length-spread warning resolved - post
  pair is 7.4% apart, capital pair 9.8%, both within the 10% tolerance.
- Observation room and .md reports now visually separate the three channels
  (table, EU room, UK room), per an explicit spec warning that mixing them
  would misrepresent what was actually said where.
- Pack changes were structural only - level, country, an empty
  privateInfo (Block 2b) slot, a capitalSeesTable flag, speaking order
  restricted to post seats. No prose authored or edited; that remains JB's
  work per the spec.

Three open decisions made unilaterally, flagged to JB:
1. Capital seats CAN read the table transcript (read-only) - JB's own
   recommendation from the spec, implemented as a pack flag so the
   alternative is a one-line change.
2. Consultation phase runs sequentially per country, not concurrently -
   isolation was the spec's requirement, ordering was left open.
3. authority fields reuse settlementTerms keys verbatim, so the mandate
   check stays mechanical.

Known gap raised, not yet fixed: the authority breach check treats a
numeric limit as a ceiling (tabling above it is a breach). That's correct
for the EU conceding volume, but backwards for the UK, where a higher TRQ is
better - a UK capital setting a floor would never trigger mandate_exceeded
under the current check. This makes the metric structurally more sensitive on
the EU side. Needs a per-term direction convention on the pack before the
breach-rate numbers are trusted.

## 11. JSON-parsing bug found and fixed

The test run above lost 6 of 52 model calls (12%) to parse failures, all
concentrated on the new Schema D/E calls - i.e. disproportionately hitting the
exact events the divergence metrics depend on. Two root causes identified by
replaying the raw failed responses:
- 4 of 6: a trailing comma before a closing brace or bracket - valid-looking
  JSON the strict parser rejected outright.
- 2 of 6: genuinely truncated/unbalanced output.

Fixed in lib/model.js: parseJson now tries progressively looser repairs
(strip fences, then strip trailing commas, then extract outermost object)
before giving up, and callModel retries once when JSON was expected but
nothing parsed. Replayed against the six real failures from the test run: 4
recovered by repair alone, the other 2 covered by the retry. Regression test
added using the actual failing payloads, not synthetic ones.
